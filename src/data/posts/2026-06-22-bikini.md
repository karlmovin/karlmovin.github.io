The word "bikini" has nothing to do with the number two. It comes from a coral reef.

On July 1, 1946, the United States detonated its first peacetime nuclear weapon at Bikini Atoll in the Pacific, part of Operation Crossroads. Four days later, on July 5, the French engineer Louis Réard unveiled a swimsuit in Paris and named it after the atoll, hoping the design would set off a reaction as "explosive" as the bomb test that was filling the world's newspapers. The name itself traces back through the German colonial spelling to the Marshallese word *Pikinni*.

Réard wasn't first to the beach. Earlier that year the designer Jacques Heim had launched a similar two-piece called the *Atome* — "the smallest bathing suit in the world." Réard answered that his was "smaller than the smallest," and quipped that he had "split the atom." The atomic theme ran through both.

The funny part is what came next. In the 1960s the designer Rudi Gernreich reinterpreted "bikini" as if it were *bi-* (Latin for "two") plus *-kini*. That back-formation was pure invention, but it stuck, and it spawned a whole family: the *monokini* (one piece), and later the *tankini* and *trikini*. A word built from a Pacific place name got retrofitted with a Latin number.

And the atoll left one more mark on pop culture. SpongeBob SquarePants lives in Bikini Bottom — an underwater city sitting beneath the very same Bikini Atoll. Its creator, Stephen Hillenburg, was a marine biologist, and the name is a direct nod to the real reef. A nuclear test site in the Marshall Islands ended up naming both a swimsuit and a cartoon sponge's hometown.
